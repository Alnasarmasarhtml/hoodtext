# HOODGRAM — NARRATIVE BIBLE

**THE BUILDING AT 4663.** Final. Not a direction among options — the direction.

This document is binding for copy, art, product strings, social, and every asset that ships. Where
this document and a designer's instinct disagree, this document wins. Where this document and
`SPEC.md` disagree, `SPEC.md` wins on facts and this document wins on how they're said.

The chosen frame beat three others (a Robin Hood legend system, a memestock-grievance system, and a
night-courier system) on all three judging lenses. It won for one reason worth restating before
anything else: **its hook is arithmetic, not a news cycle.** Robinhood Chain's ID is 4663. On a phone
keypad, 4663 spells HOOD. It also spells HOME. It also spells GOOD. A stranger can verify that in
four seconds on their own phone. Found lore is the only kind a community defends as its own, and it
has no half-life. Everything else here is built to be as durable as that number.

The best material from the three runners-up has been grafted in and is marked where it matters, so
nobody re-litigates a settled call.

## 1. THE ONE LINE

> **HoodGram is a building at 4663 — five dollars cuts you a key for life, nobody can read your mail,
> and half of every dollar this address collects goes back out to the people holding.**

**Short forms, in order of preference:**

- Five dollars. One time. The key's yours.
- An encrypted messenger with an address instead of a landlord.
- The building keeps half. The other half goes to whoever's holding.

**The line is precise and must stay precise.** It does not say "half comes back to the people who
live here." Living here costs five dollars and gets you a door. The split goes to $THOOD holders,
pro-rata, held in their own wallets through the weekly count. Most residents will hold. They are not
the same set, and we never blur them — that's the first thing a hostile reader would check.

## 2. THE STORY

*Canon lore. ~330 words. This is the text of the About page and the first thing anyone reads.*

They didn't read one message. They took the door away.

On August 3, 2026, a messenger with a billion users left every app store on earth in a few hours —
175 of them — before anyone told the people who built it. The reviewer may even have been right.
That's the part that should land. Nobody's messages were opened. The door just stopped being there.

It was allowed. Five years earlier a broker greyed out a buy button for a day, and that was allowed
too; the permission was on page nine of a document everyone had already clicked. Nobody won in 2021.
We just found out where the switch was.

Every app you talk through lives behind somebody's counter. The counter has hours. It has a manager.

So we took an address instead.

Robinhood Chain's ID is 4663. On a telephone keypad, 4663 spells HOOD. It also spells HOME. It also
spells GOOD. Check it on your own phone. Four seconds. Nobody planned that — we read the number off
the docs one night and dialed it.

4663 is a five-story walkup. Steel front door, wire glass, a mail slot, and a buzzer panel with two
round grilles that make it look like the building is looking back at you. Nobody gave us permission
to build here and nobody had to. That is not the same as nobody being able to condemn the block —
that's what the fire stairs are for.

A key is five dollars, once, forever. Every key in this building cost somebody five dollars, which is
why the hallway is quiet. Rooms are units: ten a month keeps a door open, guests
never pay, and anybody walking past can cover it. Mail is free and you never sign for it. Nothing
said in here ever comes off the walls.

Every Friday the building counts. Half of every dollar this address took in that week goes back out
to whoever was holding $THOOD when it counted. Not a promise — a number, published, including the
weeks it's embarrassing.

The counter closes. The building doesn't.

## 3. THE CHARACTER

Two pieces. One of them is never drawn. That refusal is the single highest-value design decision in
the system: a hooded human figure is simultaneously the biggest cultural hazard in this brief and the
oldest cliché in security marketing, and this construction makes it impossible rather than merely
forbidden.

### 3.1 THE DOOR AT 4663 — the mascot is a door

**Form.** Orthographic hairline elevation of a walkup entrance. Flat, front-on, no perspective, no
shading, no shadow. A steel slab door in its frame: transom above, wire-glass lite in the upper
third, a brushed mail slot at chest height, and a buzzer panel bolted to the right jamb.

**Proportions (fixed, use these).** Door leaf 3:7. Wire-glass lite occupies the top third, inset 12%
from each edge, cross-hatched with a 6×4 grid of hairlines at 45°. Mail slot sits at 42% height,
width 40% of the leaf, height 4% — a true horizontal aperture with a 1px overhanging lip. Transom
plate above the frame carries **4663** in Geist Mono, letterspaced +0.18em. Buzzer panel on the right
jamb: 1:2 vertical plate, four visible screw dots at the corners.

**The face — this is the app icon.** The buzzer panel resolves as a face at 16px and as architecture
at 1200px. Three features, in this order of importance:

1. **Two circular speaker grilles** — the eyes. Concentric hairline rings, 5 rings each, sitting
   side by side in the upper half of the plate.
2. **The mail slot** — the mouth. A horizontal aperture with the cowl lip overhanging it. That lip
   is the only "hood" that exists anywhere in this brand: hood as architecture, never as costume.
3. **THE BLANK BOSS** *(grafted — all three judges named this the best single steal available).* A
   solid circular disc of metal centred above the grilles, exactly where every modern intercom panel
   puts a camera lens. It is never milled. There is no hole. It is drawn as a filled bone circle with
   a 1px inner ring and nothing inside it. **This is the security claim rendered as anatomy instead
   of argument.** When somebody asks what the dot is, the answer is one word: no camera.

**Materials and palette.** Bone hairline stroke on void (#08090A). One weight only: 1px at 1x, 1.5px
at 2x. Never filled, never shaded, never textured, except: **brass (#8A6E3B) on hardware only** —
the four screw dots, the ring around the buzzer button, the mail-slot lip. Brass is the entire warmth
budget in the drawn objects and it is what stops the whole system reading as a terminal window.

**Behaviour — one expression, one state change.** The left grille snaps to #00C805 when someone is
buzzing and fades off over 120ms. That is the complete animation vocabulary for the character. No
blinking, no bounce, no idle loop, no personality tics.

**What it never does.** It never turns. It never gets a body, arms, legs, or a hoodie. It never
smiles — it has no mouth muscles, it has a mail slot. It is never rendered in perspective, never
photographed, never drawn in a "real" street, never given a face-like expression beyond lit/unlit.
It never appears greyed out.

### 3.2 THE SUPER — the relay, personified as an absence

**Never illustrated. No face, no body, no silhouette, no name, no backstory, ever.** He exists only
as hand-lettered index cards taped to a door, always signed `— The Super`. Every system notice in the
product is one of those cards.

He has exactly three properties, and all three are literal product facts:

- He takes the whole building's mail down in one sack, so nobody can tell whose is whose, and it
  costs you nothing. *(Batching relay; sender address never on chain.)*
- He can jam the front door. He can never open your mail and he can never sign your name.
  *(Relay can censor; cannot read; cannot forge.)*
- **He does not have a key to your unit.** The master key that exists in every real building does not
  exist in this one. *(End-to-end encryption, in one sentence a child understands.)*

**THE DUMBWAITER** *(grafted from the runner-up)* is his machine — the drawable half of the relay. A
sealed box on a rope in a shaft, that cannot open what it carries and does not know whose it is. It
gives the mail run a picture without giving the Super a body. **THE FIRE STAIRS** are self-posting:
code-mandated, ugly, always unlocked from the inside, and you hope you never need them.

**The running joke that outlives the launch: nobody has ever seen the Super.** People will post fake
sightings forever. Let them.

**Two hard character rules.**

1. **The community talks about the Super, never as him.** No roleplay accounts, no "Super says" bot,
   no first-person Super in replies. He writes notices. That's all he does.
2. **The second he gets a face, a name, or a backstory, he is a commissioned mascot and he is dead.**
   No exceptions, no anniversary art, no "here's what we imagine he looks like."

**The meme format, free on day one and requiring zero skill:** an index card, a strip of tape, one
dry line, signed `— The Super`. Anybody can make one with a real card and a phone camera. That is the
lowest participation floor of any direction considered, and it is deliberate.

**Canon house-rules card** *(the template everyone remixes — grafted, with the rules corrected to the
actual spec):*

```
        HOUSE RULES — 4663

   NO VOUCHING.
   NOTHING COMES OFF THE WALLS.
   RENT KEEPS THE DOOR OPEN.
   IF THE DUMBWAITER STOPS, TAKE THE STAIRS.

                        — The Super
```

`NO VOUCHING` is on the card, not buried in a policy page, because it is the anti-Nextdoor rule and
putting it on the meme template makes the community enforce it for us. There is no verification, no
neighbour reporting, no "new here" flag, no member directory that exposes who just arrived. The
five-dollar key is the only gate and it is blind. Money does not have an opinion about you.

## 4. THE VOICE

**Register: the building notice.** Typed on a card, taped to a door, by somebody who has explained
this before. Slightly annoyed is the correct default temperature. It talks about the building, never
about itself. Humour comes from mundane specificity, never from a punchline, and the joke is never
explained.

**Sentence shape.** Subject, verb, object, stop. Ten to fourteen words. When something is technical
the sentence gets *shorter*, not longer. Two sentences maximum before a line break — this is copy
that has to fit on a 3×5 card. Numbers stated flat and unrounded, including the embarrassing ones.

### Two editorial laws

**LAW 1 — THE TRUTH TAX.** *(Grafted.)* Every claim about safety ships with its own exception inside
the same sentence or the one immediately after, before anyone asks. Not a tone preference — a
checkable rule. If a security sentence has no cost attached to it, it isn't finished.

**LAW 2 — ONE BUILDING NOUN PER SENTENCE, MAXIMUM.** *(Grafted.)* The known failure mode of this
direction is the metaphor going decorative. Two building nouns in one sentence is how that starts.
"Rent runs out, the door stops taking new mail and the walls keep everything" is a fail. "Rent ran
out. Nothing came off the walls" is correct.

**Corollary to Law 2:** every beat must stay *literally true*. Rent day is a real payout. The fire
stairs are a real button. The Super genuinely cannot read anything. Any beat that stops being
literally true gets deleted from the language, not softened.

### Never says

revolutionizing · empowering · the future of · seamless · unstoppable · uncensorable · unmoderatable
· anonymous · untraceable · military-grade · trustless · join the movement · we're building · web3 ·
gm · wagmi · moon · diamond hands · ape · HODL · fam · the streets · "community" (say the building,
the block, or whoever lives here) · democratize (that word belongs to somebody else and it is
off-limits) · dividend · yield · passive income · APY · returns · any projection of price.

**Never an exclamation mark. Never an emoji. Never a rhetorical question. Never AAVE or any borrowed
street slang — not one word. Never first person plural as a resident** ("we from the block" is the
single fastest way to kill this). Never "hood" as an adjective for people or style; here it is a
number on a keypad and a lip over a mail slot, and nothing else.

### The ladder — FINAL

**KINGPIN is cut.** It is the one rung that steps out of civic vocabulary into narcotics-boss
cosplay, it cannot be defended the first time a journalist asks why the top tier is a drug lord, and
it dates the brand to a streaming crime drama. All three judges independently killed it.

| Floor | Name | Hold | What it opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| ground | *(a key)* | — | the door, a plate, every room you're invited to |
| 1 · `\|` | **RESIDENT** | 0.05% | your badge beside your name, everywhere |
| 2 · `\|\|` | **BLOCK CAPTAIN** | 0.1% | 4-character plates · bigger rooms · heavier uploads |
| 3 · `\|\|\|` | **DISTRICT** | 0.25% | 3-character plates · early features |
| 4 · `\|\|\|\|` | **LANDMARK** | 0.5% | 2-character plates · the sign on the roof (broadcast rooms) |

**LANDMARK is structurally the correct top rung.** Landmark status is a real civic designation: it is
conferred rather than bought, it pays nothing, it confers prestige and *restriction*, and it is
visible on the map. People give directions using you. That is a better flex than KINGPIN ever
produced, and it renders as a plaque.

**The grammar shift across the ladder is the story:** RESIDENT is a person. BLOCK CAPTAIN is a person
who speaks for a block. DISTRICT is where you stop being a person and become a jurisdiction. LANDMARK
is where you become part of the map.

**The typeable token** *(grafted — the highest-value single steal in the exercise).* Every rung has an
ASCII form: `|` `||` `|||` `||||`. A plaque is a picture and pictures don't travel in a chat display
name. Bars do. **The ask that carries it is "how many floors?"** — typeable, exclusionary, encodes the
real mechanic, and wearable in a bio the day the ladder ships.

**What the floors never buy.** Standing buys square footage. It never buys money and it never buys a
discount. Everybody pays the same five dollars and the same ten a month, LANDMARK or not. The split
is pure pro-rata at every size — a LANDMARK and a RESIDENT holding the same amount get the same
envelope. Say this out loud and often; it is both true and legally protective.

### THE NIGHT MAYOR — one honorific, outside the ladder

Every month, whoever covered the most rooms' rent is the Night Mayor. It is a real civic office
(Amsterdam, 2003; New York's Office of Nightlife), which is exactly the kind of fact people go and
check. It pays nothing, commands nothing, gates nothing, and expires in thirty days. It exists for
one reason: to make covering rent — the most generous, most repeatable, most screenshottable act in
the product — visible.

### "-GRAM" IS OURS, AND HERE'S WHY

The suffix needs its own etymology so it never reads as borrowed from anybody else's messenger. It
has two, and both are true:

- **A gram is a weight.** Mail is weighed by the gram. The split pays by weight — pro-rata by
  holdings. You get paid by yours.
- **A -gram is something written down.** *Gramma*, Greek: a thing written. Every letter in here is
  stamped into a permanent record.

Anchor "gram" to the **postal scale** — always mail, always the post office, never any other kind of
scale. That distinction is not optional.

## 6. THE PHRASES

Ranked by how likely a stranger is to repeat them without being asked.

1. **rent day** — the ritual. The most dreaded phrase in urban life, inverted into the day you get
   paid. Calendar-anchored, weekly, forever.
2. **be home for the count** — carries the anti-mercenary snapshot rule inside a sentence people will
   type without ever thinking about tokenomics.
3. **i got the room this month** — a completed action in natural speech. Ten dollars, public,
   repeatable generosity that buys visible standing. The most important sentence in the product.
4. **buzz. / come up.** — *(grafted principle: a messenger's signature phrase must take two people,
   because every use recruits a second participant.)* Two words, lowercase, and it is literally what
   a walkup does.
5. **nothing comes off the walls** — the archive promise, said as furniture.
6. **the Super doesn't have a key** — end-to-end encryption, six words, no jargon.
7. **the building keeps half. the other half goes to whoever's holding.** — the tokenomics with no
   finance word in it. *(On the token page, addressed to holders, the short form "you're the other
   half" is permitted. Nowhere else.)*
8. **a bot army here costs five dollars a head** — the spam wall as arithmetic. Immediately followed,
   always, by "that's not a security model, it's a bill."
9. **how many floors?** — the status ask. Answered in bars: `|` `||` `|||` `||||`.
10. **fire stairs are open** — the escape hatch, stated as an ordinary fact about a building.

**Bench (real, but second tier):** nobody has ever seen the Super · rent goes sideways here · the room
went quiet · nobody gave us permission to build here, and nobody had to · buzz me at 4663 · I'm on
4663 · nobody vouches for anybody here · you don't sign for mail here · don't pay me back.

**The fixed disclosure — not a phrase, a permanent string:**

## 8. THE VISUAL

Signals Desk is not restyled. It is **rebuilt as architecture** — the system already had the right
parts and no reason for them.

### Sacred — does not change

- Void **#08090A**. Bone text. Geist Mono for labels and data, Geist Sans for prose.
- Hairlines. The 6px corner notch. 3% film grain.
- **One reserved accent: #00C805.** Standing bans: no gradients, no glassmorphism, no neon, no glow,
  no emoji-as-icons.

### The green rule — the hardest discipline in the system

Green is **not a brand field. It is the lit state.** #00C805 appears on exactly three things:

1. a live buzz,
2. a rent-paid / door-open state,
3. the rent day number.

Never on a default button. Never on a heading. Never decorative. Maximum one green element per
screen. Green means *it's on*, and nothing else — which is what makes the LANDMARK plaque worth
having and what makes a lit door legible from across a room.

**And the law with the knife in it** *(grafted, verbatim, because it supplies the motive the rule was
missing):* **nothing in this product ever renders as greyed-out, because greying out is the crime.**
A room whose rent lapsed is **unlit**, never disabled. A gated feature is **upstairs**, never
disabled. This is a binding instruction to engineering: the lapsed-room composer in `apps/web` must
render as quiet, not as a disabled control.

### Palette moves

| Token | Hex | Used for | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| VOID | `#08090A` | everything under everything | — |
| BONE | bone/off-white | type, hairlines, drawn objects | — |
| LIVE | `#00C805` | the three lit states above | headings, buttons, fills, anything beside the word Robinhood |
| BRASS | `#8A6E3B` | **hardware only** — screws, buzzer ring, mail-slot lip, plate edges | type, fields, backgrounds |
| VACATE | `#8C1C13` | **one thing forever:** decisions made about us by somebody outside | anything we did |

**BRASS** *(grafted)* is the only warmth in the drawn world and it is what stops the whole thing
reading as a terminal.

**VACATE RED** *(grafted)* is the second-most-important addition here. **Red never marks anything we
did. Red means somebody with a review queue made a decision about you.** Store removals, takedowns,
delisting notices, condemnation. It renders as a DOB-style vacate placard — a red-ruled notice card,
all-caps mono — and it is completely civic with zero medieval, zero alarm-state UI. The system
previously had no way to render an outside actor. Now it does, and the day it appears it will mean
something.

### The mark, and the icon — two marks, two jobs

- **THE MARK: the 4663 doorplate.** A horizontal rounded rectangle, hairline bone stroke, one notched
  corner, `4663` in Geist Mono letterspaced. **Wordless.** It functions as an address, not a logo.
- **THE ICON: the buzzer face.** Two grilles, the mail slot with its cowl lip, and the blank boss
  where the camera isn't. Nothing else. Reads at 16px.

### Motifs

1. **The hairline is the building.** Rules read as floor plans, elevations, door frames, jamb lines.
   The 6px notch is now a clipped nameplate corner and only appears on things that would physically
   be a plate or a card.
2. **The tape.** Copy surfaces are index cards: Geist Mono at notice scale, hard-set to card measure,
   hairline card edge, and one 2px bone rectangle drawn as tape at **3°**. **That rotated rectangle
   is the only rotation permitted anywhere in the system.** It is the entire warmth budget in layout,
   and it is the detail people will imitate.
3. **Dividers that are literally data** *(grafted).* The section rule is a row of short mail-slot
   ticks. Each tick is one anchored message. The ornament is the ledger — which is how "nothing is
   ever deleted" becomes decoration that isn't decorative.
4. **Plaques as badges.** Tier marks are small rectangular plaques, two brass screw dots, hairline
   bevel, name in letterspaced Geist Mono caps. LANDMARK is the only one with a green hairline rule
   under the word. Every plaque ships with its ASCII twin (`|` `||` `|||` `||||`) so status can be
   typed as well as seen.
5. **Receipts as a typographic class** *(grafted).* Every rent day publishes a fixed-width mono ledger
   strip at identical size, always, including the weeks that read $412:
   `RENT DAY 07 · IN $18,412 · OUT $9,206 · 4,113 WALLETS · 11.14.26`
   A live receipt column runs down the site. A brand that only posts good numbers has already told
   you it will stop posting.
6. **The second grain** *(grafted).* A coarser paper grain restricted to card and receipt surfaces
   only, so printed things read printed and everything else reads rendered.
7. **The keypad easter egg.** Rendered exactly once, small, in the footer: a four-key diagram — 4 6 6
   3 — with GHI MNO MNO DEF beneath it, and the three readings set in mono: `HOOD / HOME / GOOD`.
8. **The wards drawing.** The security page carries one orthographic illustration: a key's bitting
   against the lock's wards — the ridges that block a wrong key. It is the only "how encryption
   works" diagram we ever ship.

### The repeated constant *(grafted)*

**Every clock in every marketing asset reads 03:12. Every date is a Friday.** Never noon, never a
Tuesday. It costs nothing, it is invisible until somebody notices, and noticing is the in-group's
first free reward. It also makes a year of work look like it was authored by one person.

### Illustration lane

Orthographic hairline technical drawing only: building elevations, unit floor plans, a cutaway of the
mail run, the dumbwaiter shaft, the fire stairs.

**Banned permanently:** any human figure, any hoodie or hooded silhouette, any bird / feather / arrow
/ bow / quill, graffiti or spray type, brick or chain-link texture, stoop photography, boombox,
padlock icons, keyhole-with-an-eye, binary rain, drop shadows, gradients, glass, neon, blackletter,
and any photograph of a real street.

### Motion and sound

Things **chunk**; they don't ease. State flips under 120ms (the chain does ~100ms blocks — borrow the
tempo). The only signature animation is the grille snapping green on a buzz and fading off. If sound
ships: a flat electric buzz on send, a door-strike chunk on room join. Nothing pulses. Nothing
breathes. Nothing glows.

### Family architecture

The block holds more than one building. HoodGram is the walkup at 4663. HoodStorage is the storage
place down the same block. Same address system, same hairline drawing language, different buildings,
different jobs — **we carry, they keep.**

## 9. LAUNCH BEATS

Five moments, in order. Nothing here requires a budget.

**BEAT 1 — THE NOTICE.** A single post, no logo, no link, no branding:
> They didn't read one message. They took the door away.
> 175 storefronts. A few hours. Nobody told the people who built it. The reviewer may even have been
> right — that's the part that should land.

Nothing else. No product, no ticker, no CTA. The claim is structural and unfalsifiable, which is why
it can lead.

**BEAT 2 — THE ADDRESS.** The found-lore post, and the highest-velocity asset we own. A photograph of
an actual telephone keypad with 4, 6, 6, 3 marked, and four lines: `4663 is Robinhood Chain's ID.` /
`On a keypad it spells HOOD.` / `It also spells HOME. It also spells GOOD.` / `Nobody gave us
permission to build here, and nobody had to.` Anyone can verify it in four seconds, which is exactly
why they will.

**BEAT 3 — THE DOOR.** The asset reveal. The 4663 elevation, the buzzer face, the app icon, and one
silent 6-second loop: the left grille snapping green and fading. One caption: `buzz.` The reply
everyone learns that day is `come up.` The blank boss gets no explanation — when somebody asks what
the dot is, the answer is two words: **no camera.**

**BEAT 4 — THE HOUSE RULES.** The Super's first card, shot on a real index card with real tape. The
four rules. Signed. Then the template goes out with one instruction: *card, tape, one line, sign it.*
Same day, the security page ships with the truth tax fully applied — "Nobody can read your mail.
Anybody can see the hallway light went on. We won't tell you otherwise" — plus the wards drawing, the
single-sequencer admission, and the no-forward-secrecy line stated before anyone asks. **Being first
to publish your own limitations is the whole credibility play.** Nobody has ever seen the Super.

**BEAT 5 — RENT DAY 01.** The first receipt, published at full size whatever it says, alongside the
floors: the four plaques, their ASCII twins, and the question `how many floors?`. Same post covers
what the floors never buy. This is the beat that converts a story into a mechanism, and skipping one
week of it turns this brand into the exact charge it was built against.

### Standing beats (forever, non-negotiable)

- **Every rent day**, the receipt. Same format, same size, including the ugly weeks.
- **Every relay outage**, the wordless post *(grafted)*: an unlit buzzer panel. No caption. The
  community already knows what it means, and an outage meme is how a community bonds during failure.
- **Every month**, the Night Mayor — whoever covered the most rooms' rent. Powerless, unpaid,
  expires.
- **Whenever anybody covers a stranger's room**, it renders publicly on the room's strip. Invisible
  status is dead status.

## 10. GUARDRAILS

### Legal — Robinhood Markets, Inc.

- **Never "by Robinhood." Never a partnership, endorsement, or affiliation implication.** The only
  permitted construction is factual and boring: *"Native to Robinhood Chain (chainId 4663).
  Permissionless. Not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Robinhood Markets, Inc."* — in
  the footer of every page.
- **No feather, no bird, no arrow, no bow, no quill, no hidden-arrow device, anywhere, ever.** Not as
  an easter egg, not "abstracted."
- **The token is always written `$THOOD`. Never `$HOOD`.** HOOD is Robinhood Markets' NYSE ticker.
  The word HOOD appears in exactly one context — as a keypad reading of 4663 — and never adjacent to
  a price, a chart, or a financial claim.
- Our green never appears in a lockup beside that company's name or anything feather-shaped.
- **Open action before launch: trademark clearance on the "-gram" suffix** (Telegram / Instagram
  adjacency). No judge caught it and it is live. The mitigation already exists in the brand — "gram"
  is anchored to postal weight and to *gramma*, a thing written — but get it checked, in writing,
  before any paid placement.
- Never use the word "democratize." It belongs to somebody else's marketing and using it invites the
  comparison we're avoiding.

### Legal — securities language

The 50% is described **mechanically and only mechanically**: half of every payment is paid out
pro-rata to holders. **Never** dividend, yield, passive income, APY, return, ROI, or any projection.
Status tiers pay nothing and confer no economic right, and saying so repeatedly is both true and
protective.

### Legal — the narrow claim

*(Grafted, and this is a veto, not a preference.)* **HoodGram claims availability and unreadability.
It never claims impunity.** We never market ourselves as unmoderatable, beyond consequence, or a
haven. Rooms have a named payer on the lease. The relay can refuse a run. Saying both out loud is a
feature, not a concession — an outlaw-framed messenger invites exactly the accusation that removes an
app from a store.

### Legal — the 2021 beat

Used **once**, in the lore, and nowhere else. Never names the company. Never alleges illegality — a
court dismissed the negligence claims because the customer agreement permitted the restriction, so
*"it was allowed"* is simultaneously the safer statement and the sharper one. Never triumphalist,
never "retail won" (falsifiable, and falsified), no rockets, no diamond hands, no apes. **If it ever
starts reading as a shot at the chain's operator, cut the paragraph — the lore stands without it.**

### Taste — the things that kill this instantly

- **No human figure. Ever.** No hoodie, no silhouette, no "abstract person." One shipped hooded
  figure and this becomes the Bodega failure overnight, and no amount of good copy saves it.
- **No AAVE, no borrowed street slang, no first-person street voice, no graffiti or spray type, no
  chain-link, no stoop photography, no hip-hop soundtracking.** The register is civic and
  class-coded, not race-coded: rent, door, board, key, mail, lease, super, count, plate.
- **We never claim to be a neighbourhood's voice. We're a building.** That distinction is the entire
  defence and it has to hold in every asset.
- **The Super never gets a face, a name, a backstory, or a first-person account.** The community talks
  about him, never as him.
- **No vouching mechanic, ever.** No verification, no neighbour reports, no "new here" flags, no
  arrival-exposing directory. Neighbourhood software structurally invites a
  who-doesn't-belong-here feature and ours must be incapable of one. The five-dollar key is the only
  gate and it is blind.
- **Nothing renders greyed out.** Ever. Unlit, quiet, upstairs — never disabled.
- **The metaphor never goes decorative.** One building noun per sentence. Any beat that stops being
  literally true gets deleted, not softened.

### Honesty — the encryption limits, stated by us first

These ship on the security page, in the product, in this wording, and are never softened, never
buried, and never removed:

- **Message contents are unreadable by anyone but the recipient. Metadata is minimized, not
  eliminated.** That sentence is the whole claim.
- **No anonymity against a global observer.** "Anybody can see the hallway light went on."
- **No forward secrecy.** "If somebody gets your key, they get everything behind your door —
  including what's already there. The lock doesn't change itself."
- **The relay can censor.** It can never read and never forge. The fire stairs are permissionless
  forever, and if self-posting ever stops being a real, documented, one-tap button, the brand is a
  lie.
- **Robinhood Chain is a single-sequencer L2 that can screen.** We say it ourselves. Pretending the
  address is beyond reach is the one lie that would kill this from the inside.
- **Lose your wallet, lose your history.** No recovery, no escrow, no spare key downstairs.

### The audit risk — the one that actually matters

This frame makes us auditable by design, and that is the point of it. **The only defence is
receipts.** The rent day number publishes every week, on chain, publicly, at the same size, including
the weeks it reads twelve dollars. Miss one week — pause it "for a migration," make it discretionary,
post only the good ones — and this brand becomes the exact indictment it was built against. This is a
product commitment before it is a copy commitment.

### Never blur these two sets

**Residents** are people who paid five dollars for a key. **Holders** are wallets holding $THOOD
through Friday's count. They overlap heavily and they are not the same set. Every sentence about the
split says *holders*. Getting sloppy here is the fastest way to make a true thing sound like a lie.
